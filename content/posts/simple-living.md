Describe a moment when your perception of someone completely changed. What caused it?

Writing shows me how little I know about my thinking, it is a humbling thing to do.

I had previously thought of my roommmate as a lazy, unmotivated person. The first day I met him, all he did was talk about how much he cheated in high school. Being from a high school where this culture was prominent, I was very put off of this. I had convinced myself of my own moral rightness, and I thought that I was better than him in my own moral standing. I developed a lot of negative feelings towards him as a result, and I believe that this prevented me from connecting with him on a deeper level. Maybe from a source of loneliness, laziness, or lack of proactivity, I convinced myself that this was the only option I had to room with.

I've never seen someone so openly express their jealousy towards me. I was very surprised and put off by this that I didn't really react. Mostly, I just acted very nonchalant about the whole manner. After 2 years and a bit of working time, my roommates feelings of jealousy have largely subsided. Now, I lose my vertical position of power, and I become more insecure about how I was not getting as much praise/attention/jealousy.

Honestly, I had no perspective/empathy into how he was feeling as a person. I was so caught up in my own feelings of superiority and moral righteousness that I failed to validate his feelings of jealousy and inadequacy. As time passed, this jealousy subsided, and I grew increasingly insecure as this praise disappeared. One day, after meeting an extremely sociable, extremely extroverted and successful person, I asked my roommate how he felt about this person.

There's this feeling that I suddenly get really proud of my own work, and I have trouble seeing my work from another persons perspective. I put my feelings, words, and thoughts out onto paper, and suddenly I'm blessed with this sense of accomplishment and pride that I have put something out into the world. The more powerful, the more profound, the better I feel.

Now I feel a little guilty about this.
